# Family persistence, invitations, responsibility and lifecycle (I23-I27)

V6 adds `families` and `family_memberships`; migration does not create real groups or claim existing customers. Creator identity is historical and immutable. Current responsibility points to a current membership in the same family. Leaving clears responsibility when appropriate; no successor is assigned automatically. An active group must have a current member, and a closed group must have none. Closed records and ended memberships cannot be resurrected or deleted.

Internal transaction commands create a family, append accepted membership, end membership or close a group. Callers own `BEGIN`/`COMMIT` and must roll back on error. Account/person eligibility, customer role, current session and management permissions are checked under locks. Commands require the expected family version; stale operations fail. Current responsibility or management permits removing another member; self-departure must use `leave`. A last remaining member may close a group without an accepted successor. Append-only sanitized family audit events share the caller transaction.

Invitation acceptance metadata records provenance but is not a substitute for token verification. I24 validates and redeems the actual invitation in the same transaction before joining. I25 exposes bilingual family creation, current membership and deliberate invitation review/acceptance. Membership does not grant dependent guardianship, management roles or an independent credit balance.

The user confirmed one active family per customer on October 4, 2026. V7 adds a global partial unique index on current memberships by person, preserving V6's checksum and all ended memberships. Create/join commands check this after locking the person/account; concurrent attempts cannot join two groups. Customers must leave the current group under the existing credit rules before creating or accepting another. Existing conflicting memberships cause migration failure without deleting history, choosing a group or moving credits; an operator must reconcile them explicitly.

Departure and closure require a supplied domain coordinator using the same PostgreSQL client and locks. Missing coordination fails closed; Identity never treats missing credit tables as evidence of no obligations. The coordinator must enforce purchaser ownership, reserved credits, expiration and explicit donation choices. I27 defines the integration contract without implementing credit disposition or offering a live departure workflow. After a coordinator returns, session expiry is checked again before membership mutation; failure rolls back the complete transaction.

The demo seed creates two fictional groups and three memberships: a shared group for `customer.profile` and `customer01`, and a solo group for `customer18`. Acceptance provenance is synthetic development data, with no email or financial mutation. Reseeding preserves group renames, closure and departures. Existing unassigned `customer02` and the manager/customer account provide the I24 invitation and management-assistance scenarios; no extra fictional accounts or preaccepted consent are needed. See [the demo guide](development-demo.md).

## Invitation API (I24)

All routes are under `/api/v1/families`, authenticated, private (`Cache-Control: no-store`) and isolated from public parser/error logging. POST requires an allowed Origin, the session cookie, `X-CSRF-Token` and JSON limited to 2 KB. Unknown fields are rejected. GET does not accept tokens; opening an emailed link never joins a group.

| Method/path | Request | Result/authority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `POST /` | `{name}` | 201 family summary; customer creates their own group only, including personnel with customer role |
| `GET /current` | None | `{family: summary or null}` for the signed-in customer |
| `GET /for-account/:id` | None | Customer account lookup for admin/manager only; returns its current family or null |
| `GET /:id` | None | Summary for a current independent member or admin/manager |
| `POST /:id/invitations` | `{email}` | 202 `{code: "ACCEPTED"}`; any current independent member or admin/manager |
| `POST /invitations/preview` | `{token}` | Family ID/name and expiry; only intended eligible recipient; no mutation |
| `POST /invitations/accept` | `{token}` | Updated family summary; only intended eligible recipient |
| `GET /:id/responsibility` | None | `{canNominate}` for a current member or admin/manager |
| `POST /:id/successor-offers` | `{email}` | Opaque 202; current responsible member or admin/manager nominates an eligible current independent member |
| `POST /successor-offers/preview` | `{token}` | Group ID/name and expiry; intended current member only; no authority change |
| `POST /successor-offers/accept` | `{token}` | Updated family summary; intended current member explicitly accepts responsibility |
| `POST /successor-offers/decline` | `{token}` | Unchanged family summary; intended current member declines; token is consumed |

Summaries include `id`, `name`, historical `createdByPersonId`, `responsibleMembershipId`, `status` and `version`; they contain no account emails, financial information or action tokens. Management may assist invitations/read an existing group; it cannot create a group pretending to be another customer or accept for a recipient. Leave, removal, successor and closure routes are deliberately not mounted by I24.

V8 stores the family, normalized recipient, issuing account/current membership (or management assistance), version and token ID. Issuance, encrypted delivery and `family.invitation_sent` audit are atomic. Known eligible accounts are bound to their account/person/session version. A mailbox without an account can receive an invitation, but must register, confirm and sign in before accepting; inviting never creates an account or claims its history. Ineligible existing recipients, self-invitations, existing target-group members and send limits return the same opaque acceptance response, without issuing another link. API responses do not disclose recipient account existence. Timing uniformity is not guaranteed.

Invitations expire at exactly seven days and are single use. Repeating the invitation request resends after the shared 60-second cooldown, up to five sends/hour/recipient/purpose; earlier links and pending ciphertext are invalidated. A throttled resend rolls back every attempted replacement, preserving the latest valid link. Each token binds the invitation version, rather than the family's member-count version, so another accepted invite does not invalidate unrelated invitations. Closed groups, departed/ineligible senders, obsolete tokens, mismatched recipients and known-recipient account version changes fail closed. Delivery checks the same live family/sender policy before decrypting/sending; production delivery rejects development-only issuers.

Acceptance locks all participating accounts and people in UUID order, then their roles/session, family, invitation and token. It samples invitation expiry after blocking locks and rechecks the session before applying. Token consumption, membership provenance, family version and audit commit together; any failure rolls back all of them. Concurrent redemption has one winner, and concurrent invitations to different families cannot bypass V7's one-current-family constraint. A conflict leaves the losing consent unconsumed. No shared-credit calculation or guardian authorization is performed.

Historical I23 accepted memberships (including synthetic fixtures) have no actual invitation row. V8 preserves them; it does not invent token consent or add a retroactive invitation foreign key. New public joins are reachable only through validated single-use acceptance. The database validates invitation/token binding and preserves accepted invitation history, but internal membership helpers still require a trusted caller to establish consent. Direct database access is not an invitation-acceptance API.

## Successor responsibility (I26)

V9 adds historical `family_successor_offers` without changing existing families or memberships. Only a current responsible member or admin/manager can nominate an active, verified/exempt independent customer already in the group. Nomination by email returns the same opaque response for unknown/ineligible recipients and cooldowns; no pending offer grants authority. The current responsible member cannot be their own successor. Selecting another eligible candidate cancels the old pending offer, revokes its token and scrubs pending delivery, all in the same transaction. A send-limit denial rolls this back, preserving the prior valid offer.

Offers use the existing seven-day single-use email policy, 60-second cooldown and five sends/hour/recipient/purpose. Recipients sign in, review the offer, then explicitly accept or decline. Decline consumes the link and records audit history while leaving responsibility unchanged; it does not block departure. Acceptance changes only `responsible_membership_id` and the family version. Historical creator identity, memberships, purchased credits, bookings and guardianship remain unchanged. Offer, decision, token consumption and sanitized audit commit together; audit failure rolls everything back.

Offers bind the exact target membership, recipient account/email/version, issuer account/version and family version. Any membership change, closure or intervening responsibility change invalidates the offer. A departed or ineligible issuer cannot transfer authority. The delivery worker checks the same live domain policy before decrypting an email; obsolete deliveries are cancelled and scrubbed. Redemption samples expiry after blocking locks. Final offers are immutable history; their tokens cannot be reused for the opposite decision.

The lifecycle commands remove the departed creator's authority and preserve last-member closure when no successor accepted. Pending/declined offers do not add a departure requirement. Public leave/remove/close workflows await credits-bookings integration; Identity does not bypass credit disposition through this transfer screen.

## Membership lifecycle contract (I27)

`family-lifecycle.ts` exposes only `previewFamilyLifecycleInTransaction` and `executeFamilyLifecycleInTransaction` for application composition. Lower-level membership-ending and closure mutations are private to that module. Callers supply an existing PostgreSQL transaction, authenticated context and trusted credits-bookings coordinator. They must roll back on every error. No HTTP route, UI button or production financial adapter is enabled by this step.

Preview verifies the same current session, permissions, expected family version and membership locks as execution, without membership or audit mutation. The coordinator's `inspect` must also be read-only: lock and positively establish all affected financial obligations, return a bounded opaque revision, and explicitly support ownership, reservations, original value/expiry, no refunds/restoration and transactional disposition audit. Unknown/unavailable state or incomplete support fails with `CONFLICT`. Missing financial tables never justify an empty-obligation claim. No financial balances or consent references are exposed by the preview DTO.

Execution requires `expectedFinancialRevision` from preview. It repeats authorization and inspection under locks and rejects changed family or financial revisions. `apply` receives the same client and immutable plan; its matching revision receipt is required before membership changes. Domain writes, membership history and sanitized Identity audit must commit or roll back together. Session expiry is checked again after application. An adapter must not commit independently, call remote payment services inside this transaction, swallow errors or mutate Identity membership/responsibility itself. Composition must prelock all participating accounts/people in UUID order before entering Identity, then family/membership rows, then financial rows, consistently across domain operations. Identity alone does not establish financial correctness; verify the real adapter again when credits-bookings is implemented.

| Operation | Authority | Financial policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leave | Departing independent member | Explicit `keep` returns only their eligible unused/unreserved purchased credits; `donate` leaves those credits in the group |
| Remove | Current responsible member or admin/manager, never self-removal | Defaults to return the removed purchaser's eligible credits; donation requires a consent reference independently verified as accepted by that removed purchaser |
| Close | Current responsible member, admin/manager, or sole remaining member when responsibility is vacant | Return all unused/unreserved credits, including prior donations, to original purchasers |
| Last member leaves/is removed | Same authority as departure | Closure policy overrides donation because the group is closing |

For removal donation, inspection verifies consent and application receives its reference and purchaser identity to consume/revalidate and audit that exact consent atomically. A removing actor cannot assert acceptance for the purchaser; system jobs cannot request donation. All policies preserve credits attached to valid bookings, original monetary value and expiry, consumed/expired history, and guardian authorization. Closure does not refund or restore credits. Financial revision must bind relevant locked obligations and verified consent so stale previews cannot authorize changed disposition.

The existing shared/solo family demo fixtures are sufficient for membership inspection. No new fictional balances or consent are seeded: financial behavior is demonstrated only through disposable integration fixtures with explicit trusted adapters. Those test-only adapters are not application configuration or production implementations.

## Local manual check (I25)

1. Run `npm run db:migrate:demo`, `npm run db:seed:demo` and `npm run dev:demo`.
2. On the customer frontend, sign in as `customer.profile@demo.conecta.example.test` using the demo password. Open **My family** (Spanish: **Mi familia**). The existing accepted group is shown; invite `customer02@demo.conecta.example.test`. A successful send does not assert membership or disclose whether an account exists.
3. Deliver to the local fictional inbox with `node --env-file=dev.env scripts/identity-delivery.mjs --once`. Find the invitation at `http://127.0.0.1:8026`. Open its link, sign in as the intended recipient, select **Review invitation**, then deliberately select **Accept and join**. Opening the link or reviewing it alone does not join. The fragment is removed immediately; refreshing requires reopening the email link.
4. Return home and open **My family** to see the accepted group. Reopening the consumed link shows a safe invalid-link message after review. An unrelated account cannot review/accept it.
5. On the management frontend, sign in as the demo manager. Select the customer account and **Assist family** to read its group and send an invitation. Management cannot accept on the recipient's behalf or create a group for another customer. For an unassigned account, the screen explains that the customer creates their own group.
6. An unassigned customer such as `customer03` can open **My family** and create their own group. If manually assigned already, use another unassigned fictional customer. Reseeding preserves manual groups and invitations.
7. In the seeded shared family, sign in as the original responsible `customer.profile`. Open **My family**, enter `customer01@demo.conecta.example.test` under **Successor member email**, and select **Offer responsibility**. Deliver the email with the same local delivery command. Sign in as `customer01`, open the link, review it and select **Accept responsibility** or **Decline offer**. Acceptance makes that member responsible; decline leaves the current responsible member unchanged. Management can assist from **Assist family**. Refresh the family view to recheck nomination permission. Already-transferred manual groups remain transferred when reseeding; use the current responsible account or management for another offer.

The shared dialogs use existing brand tokens and 44-pixel controls. The action page supports Spanish and English, keyboard navigation, mobile layouts and explicit loading, empty, error and pending states. An uncertain mutation result requires checking current family state before retrying; neither acceptance nor decline is automatically retried. Fresh account/email/role changes clear obsolete invitation previews. Family membership grants no dependent guardianship; leave, closure and financial disposition controls belong to later steps.

Database enforcement uses [PostgreSQL deferred constraint triggers](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/17/sql-createtrigger.html), [partial unique indexes](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/17/indexes-partial.html) and [row locks](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/17/explicit-locking.html). They permit atomic creation/closure while rejecting invalid committed membership/responsibility state.
